Source: owner (David), relayed by pre-roci. Boundary item for the M6 plan, not an in-flight change.

The record from M1 to M5: validation has cost 89 to 105 USD-eq per milestone while the build fell to about 61; in M5 validation cost one and a half times the build. The causes are rowed in the M5 feedback (items 2, 3, 6, 8, 9) and the rules are written as Cody delta 6 and KB candidates R-081 to R-086. M6 applies them from its first dispatch:

1. Contract readiness before the first dispatch, and the contract frozen for the unit: every assertion claimed by one slice and testable with its named tool; every owner input present or the assertion moved; integration-dependent assertions sequenced after integration; ambiguous wording rewritten before the build; no contract pass between dispatch and boundary.
2. A claim is a receipt: a worker's hand-off lists each claimed assertion with the exact command it ran and its output; validators replay mechanical claims and spend judgement only on behavioural and mixed ones.
3. From round 2, re-grade only the affected assertions, carrying the passed set as evidence.
4. No dismissal of a hand-off item without the assertion grep; an item that names behaviour an assertion covers is a fix.
5. Validators as leads with Sonnet seats (Rule D), and the owner will consider a cheaper model for the user-testing validator kind; scrutiny keeps judgement on Opus.
6. Owner inputs named by the contract are readiness rows with a date and an inbox nag: the reference CSV, the owner screenshots.

Record per unit: validation share of cost and time, rounds and failed rounds, assertions re-tested per round, dismissed items later failed. M6's verdict condition: validation under the build's cost.
